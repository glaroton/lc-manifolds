# lc-manifolds

By [@glaroton](https://github.com/glaroton).

**Manifolds modeled on locally convex spaces, formalized in Lean 4 and Mathlib.**

API documentation (generated by doc-gen4): <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-manifolds/docs/>
Blueprint: <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-manifolds/blueprint/>
(PDF: <https://glaroton.github.io/lc-manifolds/blueprint.pdf>)

> **Status.** The library builds with no `sorry`, and its main theorems depend only on Lean's
> standard axioms. It contains manifolds, smooth maps, the tangent bundle, vector fields with the
> Lie bracket, connections, sprays, geodesics, submanifolds and immersions, connections defined
> by fields of projectors, Finsler and pseudo-Riemannian metrics with their metric geometry,
> differential forms with de Rham theory, vector bundles (pullback, Hom, dual, cotangent) with
> connections (including induced connections on pullback and Hom bundles), curvature and bundle
> metrics, symplectic, complex and Kähler manifolds, and a collection of examples.
> Work continues. It is the geometric companion of
> [lc-analysis](https://github.com/glaroton/lc-analysis), which provides the calculus.

What might belong in Mathlib, and how it overlaps with Mathlib, is mapped in
[UPSTREAMING.md](UPSTREAMING.md).

## Convention: model spaces need not be complete

Model spaces are Hausdorff locally convex spaces, **not assumed complete**, as in Glöckner–Neeb
(following Glöckner 2002); Milnor (1984) assumed sequential completeness instead. The formal
theory does not need completeness. Results that do need it (integrals in the model space,
differential equations, exponential maps, inverse function theorems) state it as a hypothesis.

In particular, a manifold modeled on a normed space is **not** a Banach manifold unless that space
is complete, and one modeled on an inner product space is **not** a Hilbert manifold unless that
space is complete. Every statement about Banach or Hilbert manifolds here carries the
completeness hypothesis. Without completeness things go wrong: Glöckner–Neeb (Example 5.6.8)
give a Lie group modeled on an incomplete normed algebra with no exponential function.

## Contents

* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Manifold/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Manifold/Basic.lean):
  * `mbGroupoid E`, the structure groupoid of Michal–Bastiani smooth diffeomorphisms between open
    subsets of a locally convex space `E`;
  * `MBManifold`, smooth manifolds modeled on `E` [Nee06, Def. I.3.1; GN26, Ch. 3];
  * `BanachManifold`, `HilbertManifold`: the case of a complete normed, resp. complete inner
    product, model space (these name the completeness hypothesis explicitly).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Manifold/Product.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Manifold/Product.lean):
  products. `M × N` is a manifold modeled on `E × F` (`Prod.mbManifold`); the projections are smooth
  and pairs of `C^k` maps are `C^k`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Basic.lean):
  manifolds with rough boundary [GN26, §3.5], modeled on locally convex subsets with dense interior
  of `E`, with the `C^k` calculus `ContDiffLC` of lc-analysis [GN26, §1.4].
  * `IsRoughChart`, `RoughAtlas r E M`: `C^r` atlases of rough charts, `r ∈ ℕ∞`, as Mathlib
    `PartialHomeomorph`s with open sources and locally convex targets with dense interior
    [GN26, Def. 3.5.1(a)]; `RoughAtlas.maximal`, `IsMaximal`, `RoughManifold`: maximal atlases
    [GN26, Def. 3.5.1(b)];
  * `RoughAtlas.boundary`, `RoughAtlas.mem_interior_iff`, `RoughAtlas.mem_boundary_iff`: the
    boundary is well defined [GN26, Def. 3.5.1(c), Lemma 3.5.6], with analytic core
    `ContDiffLC.mem_interior_of_rightInvOn` (chain rule and Hahn–Banach);
  * `RoughAtlas.ContMDiff`, `ContMDiff.comp`, `contMDiff_maximal_iff`: `C^s` maps, composition,
    chart independence [GN26, Def. 3.5.11, Prop. 3.5.12];
  * examples: `RoughAtlas.ofChartedSpace` (manifolds, with empty boundary), `RoughAtlas.prod`
    (`∂(M × N) = ∂M × N ∪ M × ∂N`), `RoughAtlas.ofSubset`, `ofConvex`, `halfSpace`,
    `unitInterval` (boundary `{0, 1}`) [GN26, Ex. 3.5.3, 3.5.5, Rem. 3.5.7].
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Submanifold.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Submanifold.lean):
  open subsets, full submanifolds and corners of manifolds with rough boundary.
  * `RoughAtlas.restrict`, `boundary_restrict`: nonempty open subsets, with `∂U = U ∩ ∂M`
    [GN26, Ex. 3.5.4]; built on `PartialHomeomorph.subtypeRestr'`, the restriction of a

  * `RoughAtlas.IsFullSubmanifold`, `fullSubmanifold`, `boundary_fullSubmanifold`: full

  * `quadrant`, `RoughAtlas.IsWithCorners`, `IsWithBoundary`, `quadrantAtlas`,
    `boundary_quadrantAtlas`: manifolds with corners and with boundary [GN26, Rem. 3.5.10].
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Subspace.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Subspace.lean):
  submanifolds modeled on closed subspaces [GN26, Def. 3.5.14, Rem. 3.5.16].
  * `IsAdaptedTo`, `subChart`: charts with `φ(U_φ ∩ N) = V_φ ∩ F` and `V_φ ∩ F` with dense
    interior relative to `F`, and the induced charts `U_φ ∩ N → V_φ ∩ F`;
  * `RoughAtlas.IsSubmanifold`, `RoughAtlas.submanifold`: conditions (S1)–(S2), and the
    `C^r` atlas of rough `F`-charts of `N` (corestriction to `F` by lc-analysis's
    `contDiffLC_subtype_iff`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Graph.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/Graph.lean):
  graphs of maps into manifolds without boundary [GN26, Prop. 3.5.17].
  * `roughGraphChart`: the chart `(x, y) ↦ (φ x, ψ y - ψ (f x))` of `M × N`, compatible with the
    product atlas and adapted to the graph and `E × {0}`;
  * `RoughAtlas.exists_roughGraphChart`, `RoughAtlas.graphSubmanifold`,
    `RoughAtlas.boundary_graphSubmanifold`: the graph of a `C^r` map is a submanifold modeled on
    `E × {0}` (condition (S1) for all `r`, the submanifold structure for `r ≥ 1`), with boundary
    `{(x, f x) : x ∈ ∂M}`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/TangentBundle.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/RoughBoundary/TangentBundle.lean):
  the tangent bundle of a `C^r` manifold with rough boundary, `r ≥ 1` [GN26, 3.5.22, with Def.
  3.3.3–3.3.11, Lemma 3.3.8, Prop. 3.3.12].
  * `RoughAtlas.tangentBundleCore`, `RoughAtlas.TangentBundle`: `TM` as a fiber bundle core over
    the chart domains, with coordinate changes `d(ψ ∘ φ⁻¹)(φ x, ·)`; these are continuous linear
    and invertible also at boundary points (`exists_clm_tangentCoordChange`,
    `tangentCoordChange_inv`, from [GN26, Lemma 1.4.7, Prop. 1.4.10]);
  * `RoughAtlas.tangentChart`, `RoughAtlas.tangentAtlas : RoughAtlas (r - 1) (E × E) TM`: the
    tangent charts `Tφ` with targets `V_φ × E`, transition maps `T(ψ ∘ φ⁻¹)`
    (`tangentChart_trans_eqOn`, `ContDiffLCUpTo.tangent`); `boundary_tangentAtlas`:
    `∂(TM) = π⁻¹(∂M)`; `contMDiff_proj`;
  * `RoughAtlas.tangentMap`: `Tf`, chart independent (`dLC_localRep_change`), equal to
    `T(ψ ∘ f ∘ φ⁻¹)` in tangent charts (`tangentChart_tangentMap`), `C^{s-1}` for `C^s` maps
    (`contMDiff_tangentMap`), with `T(id) = id` and the chain rule `T(g ∘ f) = Tg ∘ Tf`
    (`tangentMap_id`, `tangentMap_comp`);
  * for ordinary manifolds the coordinate changes are those of `TangentBundle E M`
    (`tangentCoordChange_ofChartedSpace`); a diffeomorphism between the two tangent bundles is
    not constructed.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection/Basic.lean):
  connections and covariant derivatives. A `Connection` is a family of Christoffel symbols `Γᵢ`, one
  per chart, with the change of charts law `Γⱼ(τ x)(dτ a, dτ b) = dτ Γᵢ(x)(a, b) + d²τₓ(a, b)`;
  conventions as in Lang (first argument the direction of motion, Lang's sign for the curvature).
  * `covDeriv`: `∇_X Y = dỸ · X̃ − Γ(X̃, Ỹ)`; `localRep_covDeriv`: it does not depend on the
    chart (the `d²τ` terms cancel);
  * `covDeriv_smulField_left`, `covDeriv_smulField_right`: `∇_{φX} Y = φ ∇_X Y`,
    `∇_X (φY) = X(φ) Y + φ ∇_X Y`;
  * torsion: `∇_X Y − ∇_Y X − [X, Y] = Γ(Y, X) − Γ(X, Y)` pointwise, and
    `∇_X Y − ∇_Y X = [X, Y]` for symmetric connections (sprays);
  * `contMDiffMB_covDeriv`: `∇_X Y` is `C^k` if `X` is `C^k` and `Y` is `C^(k+1)`;
  * `curvature_eq`: **the curvature is tensorial**. `R(X, Y) Z = ∇_X ∇_Y Z − ∇_Y ∇_X Z −
    ∇_[X,Y] Z` (defined through vector fields, so it does not depend on the chart) is, in a chart,
    the trilinear expression `dΓ(v)(u, w) − dΓ(u)(v, w) + Γ(u, Γ(v, w)) − Γ(v, Γ(u, w))` (Lang,
    Prop. IX.1.2); `curvature_swap`, `curvature_bianchi`: skew-symmetry and Bianchi's identity.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection/CovariantCurve.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection/CovariantCurve.lean):
  covariant derivatives along curves and geodesics.
  * `covDerivAlong`: for a lift `β` of a curve `α`, `D_t β = μ̇ − Γ(α̃)(α̃̇, μ)` in a chart;
    `covDerivAlong_chart`: it does not depend on the chart;
  * `velocity`, `isCkLift_velocity`: the velocity of a `C^(k+1)` curve is a `C^k` lift;
  * `IsGeodesic` (`D_t α' = 0`) and `isGeodesic_iff`: the geodesic equation
    `α̃̈ = Γ(α̃)(α̃̇, α̃̇)` in every chart. Existence of geodesics is not claimed here (it needs ODE
    theory, which fails in general locally convex spaces); for Banach models see
    `Geodesic/Existence.lean` and `Geodesic/Maximal.lean`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Tangent/Double.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Tangent/Double.lean): the
  double tangent bundle `TTM = T(TM)`, with charts `((x, v), (u, w))` over charts of `M`.
  * `tangentChart_transition_apply`: the transition maps of `TM` are `(x, v) ↦ (τ x, dτₓ v)`;
  * `ddChart_transition_apply`: those of `TTM` are
    `((x, v), (u, w)) ↦ ((τ x, dτₓ v), (dτₓ u, d²τₓ(u, v) + dτₓ w))`;
  * `flip`, the canonical flip `((x, v), (u, w)) ↦ ((x, u), (v, w))`: `ddChart_flip` shows it
    does not depend on the chart (the transition maps commute with it by Schwarz), and
    `flip_flip` that it is an involution.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Geodesic/Spray.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Geodesic/Spray.lean):
  sprays, by their local parts `fᵢ(x, v)` with the change of charts law `fⱼ(τ x, dτ v) = dτ fᵢ(x, v)
  + d²τₓ(v, v)`.
  * `Connection.toSpray`: a connection gives a quadratic spray, `f(x, v) = Γₓ(v, v)`;
  * `Spray.toConnection`: a quadratic spray gives a symmetric connection, by polarization
    `Γₓ(a, b) = ¼ (f(x, a + b) − f(x, a − b))`, and `toConnection_toSpray` recovers the spray.
    The key facts: a map homogeneous of degree 2 is half its second derivative at `0` on the
    diagonal (`dMB_dMB_zero_of_homogeneous`, no regularity needed), and the second derivative
    of the transition maps polarizes.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold/Basic.lean):
  embedded submanifolds. `N ⊆ M` is embedded with model a closed subspace `S ⊆ E` if every point
  lies in a slice chart, `φ(U ∩ N) = φ(U) ∩ S`; no complement of `S` is required. Then `N` is a
  manifold modeled on `S` (`IsEmbeddedSubmanifold.mbManifold`) and the inclusion is smooth. The key
  calculus fact is `ContDiffMBOn.codRestrict` (in lc-analysis): a `C^k` map with values in a closed
  subspace is `C^k` into it.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold/Notions.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold/Notions.lean):
  weak immersions (injective differential), immersions (differential a topological isomorphism onto
  a closed subspace) and split immersions (with a continuous linear left inverse); weakly immersed,
  immersed and initial submanifolds (Glöckner–Neeb, Def. 3.3.22); split submanifolds. The inclusion
  of an embedded submanifold is an immersion and is initial
  (`IsEmbeddedSubmanifold.isInitialSubmanifold_val`, via `ContDiffMBOn.codRestrict`), and a split
  immersion for split submanifolds. The module docstring lists the infinite-dimensional subtleties:
  the cusp (Joris), the figure eight, a topologically embedded weak immersion of `ℓ²` that is not
  initial, unitary orbits, and an initial submanifold that is not an immersion.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold/ImmersionBanach.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Submanifold/ImmersionBanach.lean):
  if the domain is a Banach manifold (model complete normed) and the target is modeled on a normed
  space (complete or not), an immersion (no complement of the range assumed) reflects smoothness of
  continuous maps from manifolds modeled on any locally convex space
  (`IsImmersion.smoothMBM_of_comp`); hence an injective immersion that is a homeomorphism onto its
  image is an initial submanifold (`IsImmersedSubmanifold.isInitialSubmanifold`). The calculus is
  `LocallyConvex.Analysis.ContDiffMBOn.of_comp_of_boundedBelow`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection/Projector.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Connection/Projector.lean):
  derive and project (Larotonda, *Estructuras*, Teorema *esproyectar*). For an embedded submanifold
  `X ⊆ F` modeled on `S`, the tangent spaces `T_p X ⊆ F` (read in any slice chart), fields of
  projectors `E : X × F → F` onto them (`ProjectorField`), and the connection they define
  (`ProjectorField.connection`): in a slice chart with `ψ = φ⁻¹|_S`, `Γ_y(a, b) = - dφ (E (d²ψ_y(a,
  b)))`. It is symmetric by Schwarz, and the change of charts law follows from the second-order
  chain rule (`LocallyConvex.Analysis.dMB_dMB_comp`). The covariant derivative is the projection of
  the ambient derivative (`dMB_param_localCovDeriv`), and the geodesics are the curves with normal
  acceleration, `E_α (α̈) = 0` (`connection_isGeodesic_iff`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Finsler.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Finsler.lean):
  Finsler seminorms (continuous, subadditive, positively homogeneous; not necessarily symmetric nor
  definite) and two notions on a manifold, kept apart by name. A **Finsler metric** is continuous on
  the tangent bundle; a **Finsler length structure** is only continuous along `C¹` curves, which is
  what lengths need and what quotient metrics on homogeneous spaces satisfy (Larotonda, Forum Math.
  2019, Lemma 3.19). Every Finsler metric is a Finsler length structure, because velocities of `C¹`
  curves are continuous in `TM` (`continuous_velocity`). Length of curves and the Finsler distance
  are defined for Finsler length structures, the distance over piecewise `C¹` paths (chains of `C¹`
  arcs, `Reach`). It satisfies the **triangle inequality** (`dist_triangle`), so it is an extended,
  possibly asymmetric, pseudo-distance, and it is symmetric when `μ` is reversible (`dist_comm`, by
  reversing curves).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/LengthMetric.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/LengthMetric.lean):
  metric spaces after Gromov (Larotonda, *Estructuras*, Cap. 8). Lengths of continuous curves
  (`eVariationOn`), the rectifiable distance `d_ℓ ≥ d` and the criterion for equal topologies (Lema
  8.1.8), interior metric spaces and approximate midpoints.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/HopfRinow.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/HopfRinow.lean):
  the metric Hopf–Rinow theorem (*Estructuras*, Teorema 8.2.3): in a complete, locally compact
  interior space closed balls are compact and any two points are joined by a shortest curve;
  Lipschitz reparametrization by arc length (Obs. 8.1.5).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/AsymmDist.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/AsymmDist.lean),
  [`MetricGeometry/ForwardLength.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/ForwardLength.lean),
  [`MetricGeometry/ForwardHopfRinow.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/MetricGeometry/ForwardHopfRinow.lean):
  asymmetric distances after Mennucci (Anal. Geom. Metr. Spaces 2013), with the topology of the
  symmetrized distance: forward lengths, continuity of the running length, forward-interior spaces,
  and shortest forward curves under compactness and bounded asymmetry.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerMetricSpace.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerMetricSpace.lean):
  for a reversible Finsler length structure, `(M, d_b)` is a pseudo-metric space in which `C¹`
  curves are continuous, `ℓ(γ) ≤ L_b(γ)`, and which is an interior metric space (*Estructuras*,
  Prop. 9.1.2).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerSymmetrization.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerSymmetrization.lean):
  the symmetrization `μ(v) + μ(-v)`; with a reversibility constant it gives the same topology as
  `d_b`, but not the same lengths or shortest curves.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerBounded.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerBounded.lean):
  Finsler structures bounded above and below on a normed model (*Estructuras*, Def. 9.1.3). Leaving
  a chart costs length (complete model, Hausdorff manifold; completeness cannot be dropped, see
  `Examples/IncompleteFinsler.lean`); `d_b` separates points and every open set of `M` is `d_b`-open
  (Lema 9.1.8); `d_b ≤ ‖φ y − φ x‖ / m` for structures bounded above, so strong Finsler manifolds
  are homeomorphic to `(M, d_b)` (Cor. 9.1.9); in finite dimension every Finsler metric is strong
  (Teorema 9.1.5). On non-normable models strong Finsler structures do not exist.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerHopfRinow.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerHopfRinow.lean):
  Hopf–Rinow for finite-dimensional Finsler manifolds (*Estructuras*, Cor. 9.1.10): if `(M, d_b)` is
  complete, any two points are joined by a continuous shortest curve.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerGeodesics.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerGeodesics.lean):
  Proposición 9.2.1 of *Estructuras*, `1 ⇒ 2`, for one geodesic: on a Hausdorff Banach manifold with
  a connection and a Finsler length structure bounded below, complete for the symmetrized `d_b`, a
  geodesic on `(a, b)` with bounded speed and backward speed extends past both ends
  (`Connection.IsGeodesicOn.exists_extend_of_complete`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerExp.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerExp.lean):
  Proposición 9.2.1 of *Estructuras* (p. 251), `1 ⇒ 2 ⇒ 3 ⇒ 4`. Under the same hypotheses, with
  speeds bounded along every geodesic on a bounded interval (as for geodesics of constant speed),
  every maximal geodesic is defined on `ℝ` (`Connection.maxInterval_eq_univ_of_complete`), `D(exp) =
  TM` (`Connection.expDomain_eq_univ_of_complete`) and `Dom(exp_p) = T_pM`
  (`Connection.exists_expDomainAt_eq_univ_of_complete`). The converses are not claimed: the
  Hopf–Rinow theorem fails in infinite dimension (Atkin, Bull. London Math. Soc. 7 (1975), Lemma 6),
  and complete Riemannian Hilbert manifolds may have points joined by no shortest curve (McAlpin's
  ellipsoid, *Estructuras*, Ejemplo 10.4.2).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerQuotient.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/FinslerQuotient.lean):
  where the two continuity conditions come from, for a field `μ` on `M` controlled through a `C¹`
  map `π : N → M` (typically a quotient metric). If curves lift locally and `μ ∘ Tπ` is continuous
  along curves, `μ` is a Finsler length structure; if `Tπ` is a quotient map and `μ ∘ Tπ` is
  continuous, `μ` is a Finsler metric. Includes the chain rule `Tπ(Γ̇) = (π ∘ Γ)˙`
  (`tangentMap_velocity`) and a discussion of Banach submersions versus homogeneous spaces of
  locally convex Lie groups.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Bilinear.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/Bilinear.lean):
  smooth fields of symmetric bilinear forms, by signature. **Weak Riemannian** metrics (Schmeding,
  Definition 4.1; "weak" refers to the topology of the fibers, `IsStrongAt` to the strong case) are
  Finsler metrics, via Cauchy–Schwarz (`WeakRiemannianMetric.toFinslerMetric`). **Fundamental
  decompositions** `E₊ ⊕ E₋` and the **index**, well defined as an algebraic dimension without any
  completeness (`FundamentalDecomposition.rank_neg_eq`). **Weak pseudo-Riemannian** metrics of
  arbitrary index (finite: Pontryagin type; infinite: Krein type) are weakly nondegenerate; **weak
  Lorentzian** metrics have index `1`, and a timelike vector with positive definite orthogonal gives
  a topological decomposition `ℝ v ⊕ v^⊥` (`FundamentalDecomposition.ofTimelike`). Lorentzian and
  pseudo-Riemannian metrics are not Finsler metrics.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/LeviCivita.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/LeviCivita.lean):
  metric connections for fields of symmetric bilinear forms of any signature. The **Koszul formula**
  (`Connection.koszul`), **uniqueness** of the Levi-Civita connection for weakly nondegenerate
  metrics, in particular weak pseudo-Riemannian metrics of any index (`Connection.Γ_eq_of_isMetric`,
  `WeakPseudoRiemannianMetric.covDeriv_eq`), and the **metric spray**: a symmetric connection is
  metric if and only if its spray satisfies `G(F(v), z) = −∂G(v)(v, z) + ½ ∂G(z)(v, v)` in every
  chart (`Connection.isMetric_iff_spray`; Larotonda, *Estructuras*, Teorema 10.1.1). Existence is
  not claimed: for weak metrics the Koszul functional need not be represented
  (`exists_symm_compatible_iff`; Schmeding's Example 4.22 on `ℓ²`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/NoLeviCivita.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/NoLeviCivita.lean):
  the obstruction happens. For a symmetric positive definite operator `T` and `v ∉ T(H)`, the weak

* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/ProjectorMetric.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Metric/ProjectorMetric.lean):
  submanifolds of a space with a continuous symmetric form `β` of any signature (inner product,
  Krein, Pontryagin). If the field of projectors is `β`-orthogonal, its connection is metric for the
  induced form (`ProjectorField.isMetric_symb`, Gauss) and is the Levi-Civita connection
  (`ProjectorField.symb_eq_of_isMetric`). The induced forms glue to a metric on `X`
  (`ProjectorField.inducedMetric`, weak Riemannian when `β` is positive definite), and globally
  every symmetric metric connection of it is the projector connection
  (`ProjectorField.covDeriv_eq_connection`). Orthogonal projectors only exist onto nondegenerate
  subspaces (`nondegenerate_of_orthogonal`), which is what fails for lightlike submanifolds; in
  Krein spaces the exact condition is Bognár, Theorem V.3.4 (lc-analysis,
  `Krein/OrthoComplemented.lean`).

* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/DiffCircle.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Examples/DiffCircle.lean):
  `Diff⁺(S¹)`, through its lifts `id + u`, as the open subset `Ω = {u | 1 + u' > 0}` of `C^∞_per`

* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField/Basic.lean):
  vector fields and the Lie bracket.
  * `localRep_transition`: local representatives transform by `X̃ⱼ ∘ τ = dτ · X̃ᵢ`;
  * `lieBracket`: defined in the preferred chart at each point by the local formula
    `dỸ · X̃ − dX̃ · Ỹ`;
  * `localRep_lieBracket`: **the bracket does not depend on the chart**. In infinite dimensions
    vector fields need not have flows, so the usual proof through the Lie derivative is not
    available; the proof is direct (`localBracket_transition`): differentiating
    `X̃ⱼ ∘ τ = dτ · X̃ᵢ` produces `d²τ(X̃ᵢ, Ỹᵢ)`, which cancels by the symmetry of second
    derivatives;
  * `lieBracket_swap`: antisymmetry;
  * `contMDiffMB_of_localRep`: a vector field with `C^k` local representatives is `C^k` as a map
    `M → TM`; `contMDiffMB_lieBracket`: if `X, Y` are `C^(k+1)`, then `[X, Y]` is `C^k`;
  * `lieBracket_jacobi`: the Jacobi identity for `C^2` fields (`localBracket_jacobi` in a chart:
    the second derivatives cancel by symmetry, the first-order terms by linearity);
  * `lieBracket_related`: if `X, Y` are `f`-related to `X', Y'` (`Tf ∘ X = X' ∘ f`), then
    `[X, Y]` is `f`-related to `[X', Y']` (Lang, Prop. V.1.4), for `f` of class `C^2`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField/Derivation.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField/Derivation.lean):
  vector fields acting on functions.

    `vfDeriv_chart`: it does not depend on the chart; `vfDeriv_mul`: Leibniz rule;
  * `lieBracket_smulField`: `[X, φY] = X(φ) Y + φ [X, Y]`;
  * `eq_of_vfDeriv_clm_comp_chart`: a vector field is determined at `x` by its action on the

  * `localCompat_sections`: `X h(s, t) = h(∇_X s, t) + h(s, ∇_X t)`;
  * `IsMetric.curvature_skew`: the curvature of a metric connection is skew-adjoint.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Bundle/Induced.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Bundle/Induced.lean):
  induced connections.
  * `BundleConnection.pullback`: the pullback connection `f*∇` on `f*V`, with symbols
    `Γᵢ(F y)(dF(y) a, ξ)` for the local representative `F` of `f`;
  * `BundleConnection.hom`: the connection on `Hom(V₁, V₂)` (normed fibers), with symbols
    `Γ₂(a, A ·) − A Γ₁(a, ·)`; smooth for the operator norm by `contDiffMBOn_of_unc_clm`, and its
    gauge law uses `∂(g⁻¹) = −g⁻¹ (∂g) g⁻¹` (`hom_transition`);
  * `BundleConnection.trivial`, `BundleConnection.dual`: the trivial connection on `M × F` and the
    dual connection on `V'`, `Γ'(a, α) ξ = −α(Γ(a, ξ))` (`dual_Γ_apply`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Bundle/Refinement.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Bundle/Refinement.lean):
  refinements of trivializing covers.
  * `TrivializationCover.Refines`, `MBVectorBundleCore.refine`, `BundleConnection.refine`: a
    bundle and a connection written in the trivializations of a refinement;
  * `TrivializationCover.inf`: the common refinement `Uᵢ ∩ Vₖ` of two covers; `homCoreInf`,
    `BundleConnection.homInf`: Hom bundles and their connections for bundles trivialized over
    different covers.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Complex/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Complex/Basic.lean):
  Nijenhuis operators, almost complex structures, complex manifolds and Kähler structures.
  * `EndomorphismField`, `nijenhuisLoc`, `localBracket_nijenhuis`,
    `EndomorphismField.lieBracket_nijenhuis`: the Nijenhuis torsion
    `Ω_N(X, Y) = N[NX, Y] + N[X, NY] − [NX, NY] − N²[X, Y]` is a tensor, with the local formula
    of Goliński–Larotonda–Tumpach (Rend. Lincei Mat. Appl., Theorem 3.2);
  * `AlmostComplexStructure`, `canonicalJ`, `canonicalJ_isIntegrable`: on a complex analytic
    manifold, multiplication by `i` is well defined (the tangent maps are complex linear) and
    integrable;
  * `HermitianStructure.fundamentalForm`, `KaehlerStructure.symplecticForm`: `ω(u, v) = g(J u, v)`
    is a (weak) symplectic form for a (possibly indefinite) Kähler structure; `flatKaehler`: the
    flat Kähler structure of a complex inner product space, `ω(u, v) = Im ⟨u, v⟩`;
  * Newlander–Nirenberg: its real analytic case on Banach manifolds is in
    `Complex/NewlanderNirenberg.lean` (below); it fails for smooth structures on some Banach
    manifolds (Patyi) and on some Fréchet manifolds (Lempert 1998).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Complex/NewlanderNirenberg.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Complex/NewlanderNirenberg.lean):
  the Newlander–Nirenberg theorem for real analytic almost complex structures on Banach manifolds.
  * `AlmostComplexStructure.IsRealAnalytic`, `AlmostComplexStructure.IsHolomorphicChart`: real
    analytic structures, and charts into complex spaces whose differential is complex linear for
    `J`;
  * `AlmostComplexStructure.exists_holomorphic_chart`: an integrable real analytic `J` on a
    manifold modeled on a real Banach space has a `J`-holomorphic chart around every point, into
    `ker(J(x₀)_ℂ − i)` (from lc-analysis's local theorem, after Penot, Théorème 3.5);
  * `IsHolomorphicChart.complexAnalyticOn_transition`: the transition maps between `J`-holomorphic
    charts are complex analytic.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/Basic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/Basic.lean):
  symplectic manifolds, with weak and strong nondegeneracy.
  * `SymplecticForm`, `SymplecticForm.IsStrong`; `IsStrong.surjective_inclusionInDoubleDual`: a
    normed model of a strong symplectic manifold is reflexive;
  * `IsHamiltonianFor`, `IsSymplecticField`: Hamiltonian fields are unique and symplectic;
    symplectic fields are Hamiltonian on smoothly contractible manifolds; brackets of symplectic
    fields are Hamiltonian; conservation of energy;
  * `poisson`, `poisson_jacobi`: the Poisson bracket and the Jacobi identity;
  * `IsSymplecticMap`; `canonicalSymplectic`: `ω = dθ` on `H × H` for a real inner product space
    `H`, strong when `H` is complete.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/Cotangent.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/Cotangent.lean):
  **the canonical symplectic form on the cotangent bundle** of a manifold modeled on a normed space
  `E` (no completeness) [Lan99, §V.7].
  * `cotangentLiouville`: the canonical `1`-form `θ_λ(z) = λ(Tπ z)`, a smooth `1`-form on `T*M`;
    `cotangentLiouville_localRep`: in every chart of `T*M` it is `((y, μ), (u, ψ)) ↦ μ(g(y) u)`,
    and `μ(u)` in the preferred chart [Lan99, Proposition V.7.1];
  * `cotangentSymplectic`: `ω = -dθ`, `ω((u₁, ψ₁), (u₂, ψ₂)) = ψ₂(u₁) - ψ₁(u₂)` [Lan99, Proposition
    V.7.2], exact and weakly nondegenerate for every normed model (Hahn–Banach);
  * `cotangentSymplectic_isStrong_iff`: for nonempty `M`, `ω` is strong **iff `E` is reflexive**;
    so `T*M` is only weakly symplectic for models such as `ℓ¹` or `c₀`.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/Darboux.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/Darboux.lean):
  **the Darboux theorem for strong symplectic forms on Banach manifolds**, by Moser's method
  (Weinstein, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 75 (1969), §1, Theorem; [Lan99, Theorem V.8.1]).
  * `Darboux.exists_darboux_local`: on an open set of a Banach space `E`, a smooth closed `2`-form
    `Ω : U → L(E, E')` with `Ω(p) : E → E'` bijective is constant in suitable coordinates: there is
    a local diffeomorphism `ψ` with `ψ(p) = p`, `dψ(p) = id` and `ψ*Ω = Ω(p)`;
  * `DifferentialForm.exists_darboux_chart`, `SymplecticForm.IsStrong.exists_darboux_chart`: if
    a closed `2`-form on a manifold modeled on a Banach space is strongly nondegenerate at `x₀`,
    there is a chart of the maximal atlas around `x₀` in which it has constant coefficients
    (no Hausdorff hypothesis on the manifold);
  * the steps: a primitive `α` of `Ω - Ω(p)` with `α(p) = 0`, `dα(p) = 0` from the Poincaré lemma
    (`Darboux.exists_primitive`); the time-dependent field `Ωₜ(Xₜ, ·) = -α` integrated up to time
    `1` through the maximal flow of the autonomous field `(1, Xₜ)` on an open subset of `ℝ × E`
    (`Darboux.exists_flow_of_stationary`); Moser's computation in the chart, where Cartan's
    formula becomes `Darboux.moser_identity`.
  * Strong nondegeneracy is needed (Marsden, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 32 (1972): Darboux fails for
    weak symplectic forms on Hilbert space), and with it reflexivity of the model follows; the
    module docstring lists where completeness, the operator norm and strong nondegeneracy enter.
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/DarbouxAnalytic.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/Symplectic/DarbouxAnalytic.lean):
  **the real analytic Darboux theorem**: on a real analytic Banach manifold, the Darboux chart of a
  real analytic closed `2`-form, strongly nondegenerate at `x₀`, can be chosen in the real analytic
  atlas.
  * `Darboux.exists_primitive_realAnalytic`: the radial primitive
    `α(y) = ∫₀¹ s Θ(p + s(y - p))(y - p) ds` of a closed real analytic `Θ` is real analytic near
    `p` (it is the time-`1` value of a real analytic ODE, so real analytic flows replace real
    analytic parametric integrals);
  * `Darboux.exists_darboux_local_realAnalytic`: for `Ω : U → L(E, E')` real analytic, the map `ψ`
    and `ψ⁻¹` are real analytic: Moser's field is real analytic (inversion is), its flow up to time
    `1` is real analytic (`Flow/Analytic.lean`), and `ψ⁻¹ = Fl(-1, (1, ·))` for the suspended flow,
    so no real analytic inverse function theorem is needed;
  * `DifferentialForm.exists_realAnalytic_darboux_chart`,
    `SymplecticForm.IsStrong.exists_realAnalytic_darboux_chart`: real analytic Darboux charts for
    a real analytic (`DifferentialForm.IsRealAnalytic`) closed `2`-form. In a chart, `ω` is then
    real analytic as a map into `L(E, E')` for the operator norm
    (`DifferentialForm.IsRealAnalytic.exists_clm_localRep`, by lc-analysis's
    `RealAnalyticOn.of_unc_clm₂`).
* [`LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField/Frobenius.lean`](LocallyConvexManifolds/VectorField/Frobenius.lean):
  **the Frobenius theorem on Banach manifolds** [Lan99, Ch. VI, Thms. 1.1 and 1.2, pp. 156–158].
  * `Distribution`, `Distribution.IsSmoothSplit`, `Distribution.IsInvolutive`: a distribution
    `D x ⊆ T_x M`; smooth and split of type `(E₁, E₂)` when, read in a chart and through
    `ℓ : E ≃ E₁ × E₂`, it is the field of graphs of a smooth `f : ℓ(W) → L(E₁, E₂)`; involutive
    for vector fields that are `C¹` only near the point (there need not be bump functions, so
    global fields would not suffice);
  * `Distribution.IsInvolutive.frob`: involutivity gives Penot's condition (Frob) for `f`, through
    the graph fields `ℓ⁻¹(a, f a)` extended by `0` and lc-analysis's `lieBracket_mem_graph_iff`;
  * `Distribution.IsInvolutive.exists_flat_chart`: for `E₁`, `E₂` Banach, a smooth split involutive
    distribution is constant, equal to `ℓ⁻¹(E₁ × 0)`, in a chart of the maximal atlas around every
    point (from lc-analysis's `exists_frobenius_chart`, after Penot);
  * `Distribution.isInvolutive_of_flat`, `Distribution.isInvolutive_iff_flat`: conversely, a
    distribution with flat charts is involutive (no completeness or splitting needed), so for
    smooth split distributions with Banach fibre and complement, involutive ⇔ flat charts.

There is no `sorry`.

## Design

Mathlib's `ChartedSpace` and `StructureGroupoid` accept an arbitrary topological model space, so
Mathlib's chart machinery is reused directly. A manifold modeled on `E` is a `ChartedSpace E M`
with `HasGroupoid M (mbGroupoid E)`. Only the notion of smoothness changes: Mathlib's
`contDiffGroupoid` needs a normed model with corners, and this library replaces it with the
Michal–Bastiani calculus of lc-analysis.

## What is new here and what is not

**The mathematics is not ours.** The definitions and theorems are taken from the literature
(mainly Glöckner–Neeb, Neeb, and G. Larotonda, *Estructuras geométricas para las variedades de
Banach*), and each file and the blueprint cite the source of each result,
with theorem and page numbers checked against the source wherever we could consult it.

**Where the formal proofs depart from the cited sources.** As in lc-analysis, we list the places
where the Lean proof does not follow the source, without claiming that these arguments are new;
we have not searched the literature systematically, and references are welcome.

* Homotopies are smooth maps `ℝ × M → N` rather than `[0, 1] × M → N` (there are no manifolds
  with boundary yet), and the homotopy formula is proved in charts, by differentiating under the
  integral sign, instead of through Cartan's formula as in Glöckner–Neeb.
* Hopf–Rinow for length spaces (Cap. 8 of *Estructuras geométricas*): compactness of closed balls
  is proved through total boundedness instead of a diagonal argument; the enlargement step uses
  a Lebesgue number instead of the boundary sphere; shortest curves are obtained as cluster
  points of Lipschitz curves in the pointwise topology (Tychonoff), avoiding Arzelà–Ascoli and
  the lower semicontinuity of the length. The forward (asymmetric) version is proved the same
  way.
* Approximate midpoints give curves as pointwise limits of dyadic step functions, instead of
  extending a Lipschitz map from the dyadic rationals (Lema 8.1.9).
* Induced connections on pullback and `Hom` bundles, and on common refinements of trivializing
  covers, are defined by their local symbols; the formulas are the classical ones, written in
  the generality the formalization needs.

* Local additions (`LocalAddition/Basic.lean`; Glöckner–Neeb, Definition 3.8.11) are bundled through
  the map `θ = (π, Σ)` as an open partial homeomorphism `TM → M × M` that is smooth with smooth
  inverse, and the identification `T(M₁ × M₂) ≅ TM₁ × TM₂` used in Lemma 3.8.17 is constructed
  explicitly, through the product of the tangent coordinate changes.

* The manifold of continuous maps `C(K, N)` (`MappingSpace/Basic.lean`; Glöckner–Neeb, Theorem 4.2.2
  for ℓ = 0) is packaged as a `VaryingAtlas`, since Mathlib's `ChartedSpace` has a single model
  space while the models `Γ_f` vary with `f`. Glöckner and Neeb give `Γ_f` the topology induced by
  `C(K, TN)` and obtain local convexity from the sections of the pullback bundle `f*TN` (Lemma
  4.1.31); here the topology is defined by the coordinates of sections in the charts of `N` over
  compact pieces of `K`, and shown to coincide with the one induced by `C(K, TN)`. The
  smoothness of the transition maps is proved by reducing, through closed embeddings into
  products, to finitely many compact pieces on which both maps lie in single charts of `N`, where
  the transition is a superposition operator, smooth by Proposition 1.7.1; Glöckner and Neeb argue
  through the exponential law for sections. We do not claim these arguments are new.

* The manifolds `C^ℓ(M, N)`, `ℓ ≥ 1` (`MappingSpace/Ck.lean`; Glöckner–Neeb, Theorem 4.2.2). The
  mapping spaces are built over smooth manifolds without boundary, so `M` is smooth, not a `C^ℓ`
  manifold with rough boundary as Glöckner and Neeb allow. The compact-open `C^ℓ` topology of
  `C^ℓ(M, N)` (their Definition 4.1.2, through the maps `T^j g`) is defined through jets of local
  representatives on compact sets in charts, and the topology of `Γ_f` (induced by `C^ℓ(M, TN)`)
  through local representatives in charts, the description they use for vector-valued maps
  (Definition 4.1.16, Lemma 4.1.17) and in Proposition 4.1.26; the agreement with Definition 4.1.2
  is proved separately (`MappingSpace/TopologyJet.lean`). The continuity of the charts is proved in
  the chart topology (open subsets of compact sets in charts), which is the jet topology for compact
  `M`. The transition maps are smooth because, locally, they are superposition operators `γ ↦ h ∘ γ`
  on spaces `C^ℓ(U, E)` with `h` smooth (`LocallyConvexAnalysis/MappingSpace/CkSuperposition.lean`,
  proved by induction on `ℓ` from the chain rule `d(h ∘ γ) = dh ∘ (γ ∘ pr₁, dγ)` and Proposition
  1.7.1); Glöckner and Neeb use the exponential law for sections (Proposition 4.1.34) instead. We do
  not claim these arguments are new.

* Manifolds with rough boundary (`RoughBoundary/Basic.lean`; Glöckner–Neeb, §3.5) are not Mathlib
  charted spaces, whose charts have open targets: a `RoughAtlas` is a set of Mathlib
  `PartialHomeomorph`s with open sources and locally convex targets with dense interior. In the
  proof of the invariance of the boundary (Lemma 3.5.6) only the one-sided difference quotients at
  `0⁺` of `t ↦ λ(κ(y + t u))` are used, with the direction `z − a` for an interior point `a`; chart
  independence of `C^s` maps is deduced from the composition rule (Proposition 3.5.12) by composing
  with the identity between an atlas and its maximal atlas. Example 3.5.3(b) is stated there for
  locally convex subsets with nonempty interior; we assume dense interior, which is what makes the
  inclusion a rough chart (equivalent for convex sets). The tangent bundle
  (`RoughBoundary/TangentBundle.lean`) is a `FiberBundleCore` over the chart domains instead of the
  set of equivalence classes of Definition 3.3.5 with the final topology, and the chart independence
  of tangent maps is proved by computing `d(ψ' ∘ f ∘ φ⁻¹)` in two ways with the chain rule on a
  common domain with dense interior. We do not claim these arguments are new.

* Atlases with varying models (`Manifold/VaryingAtlas.lean`) are kept as atlases, not completed to
  maximal atlases as in Glöckner–Neeb, Definition 3.2.13: smooth structures are compared through the
  relation "the identity is smooth both ways" (`VaryingAtlas.Equivalent`), shown equivalent to the
  compatibility of the two atlases.

* The chain rule for `C^{k,ℓ}` maps (`Manifold/Ckl.lean`; compare Glöckner–Neeb, Propositions 1.7.26
  and 1.7.28, proved there with Faà di Bruno's formula) is reduced to the exponential law and the
  smoothness of superposition operators, at the cost of assuming the model of the second factor
  locally compact, which holds for the manifolds of mappings `C^ℓ(M, N)` with `M` compact. We do
  not claim this argument is new.

* The exponential law for `C^ℓ(M, N)` (`MappingSpace/ExponentialLaw.lean`; Glöckner–Neeb, Theorem
  4.2.2(a)) is proved directly in charts, from the smoothness of superposition operators and the
  exponential law of lc-analysis, instead of through the exponential law for sections
  (Proposition 4.1.34). The statements assume only that `M` is compact (and Hausdorff): when `M`
  is nonempty its model is locally compact, hence finite-dimensional by Riesz's theorem, so `M` is
  modeled on some `ℝⁿ` and only `N` may be infinite-dimensional; the proofs use only the local
  compactness (for uncurrying) and treat `M = ∅` separately. Manifolds with rough boundary are out
  of scope. We do not claim these arguments are new.

* Theorem 4.2.2(b) (`MappingSpace/TopologyJet.lean`; also `MappingSpace/Topology.lean`,
  `MappingSpace/TopologyGN.lean` for the chart topology): the topology of `C^ℓ(M, N)` is defined
  through charts, and its agreement with the topology of Glöckner–Neeb, Definition 4.1.2
  (through the iterated tangent maps `T^j f`), is proved by induction on `ℓ`, both topologies
  satisfying the recursion of their Lemma 4.1.6; Glöckner and Neeb get (b) by construction.
  Iterated tangent bundles are built over a bundled structure `MBMfld.{u, v}` (model in `Type u`,
  carrier in `Type (max u v)`, a universe stable under `T`, `u` the universe of `M` and of the
  models). A target `N` in any other universe is handled by a transport along `ULift`
  (`MappingSpace/TopologyULift.lean`): `ULift N` is a manifold with the charts of `N`,
  `C^ℓ(M, N) ≃ₜ C^ℓ(M, ULift N)`, and Definition 4.1.2 is natural under diffeomorphisms of the
  target, so the transported topology is that of Definition 4.1.2 whenever both make sense. The
  theorems hold for `N` in every universe. We do not claim this argument is new.

* The topology of `C^ℓ(M, N)` is the jet topology, which uses jets on compact sets in charts
  themselves, like Hirsch's weak `C^r` topology; the chart topology (local representatives on open
  subsets of compact sets in charts, kept as `CkMap.chartTopology`) would collapse to the `C^0`
  topology when the model of `M` is not locally compact, since compact sets then have empty
  interior, and agrees with the jet topology for locally compact models
  (`CkMap.chartTopology_eq_jetTopology`). The jet topology is compared with Glöckner–Neeb,
  Definition 4.1.2, by the same induction on `ℓ` through `T`, in the charts `tangentChart j j` only,
  for families of charts covering `M × N`; the jets of `dF` and of `F` determine each other by the
  identities behind [GN26, Lemma 4.1.12]. Recovering the compact-open topology of `C(T^n M, T^n N)`
  from compact sets in charts needs every compact set of `T^n M` to be a finite union of compact
  sets in charts: true for Hausdorff `M` (Glöckner–Neeb's manifolds are Hausdorff, Def. 3.1.4) and
  for locally compact models. We do not claim these arguments are new.

* Tangent spaces of atlases with varying models (`Manifold/VaryingAtlas.lean`) are the models of
  preferred charts (kinematic tangent vectors), not classes of curves as in Glöckner–Neeb; for
  Theorem 4.2.2(d) (`MappingSpace/Tangent.lean`) `Θ` is computed by the chain rule at the zero
  section for a normalized local addition, and reduced to that case through the normalization of
  [GN26, Remark 3.8.16] for an arbitrary one. In infinite dimensions these tangent spaces need not
  agree with spaces of derivations (Kriegl–Michor, *The Convenient Setting of Global Analysis*, 28.1
  and Lemma 28.4). We do not claim these arguments are new.

**What is new is the formalization**, together with the bookkeeping that infinite dimensions
impose on it: model spaces that need not be complete, explicit trivializing covers, and the
distinction between the notions that coincide in finite dimension (flagged in each file).

## Using the library

Add the dependency to the `lakefile.toml` of a Lake project:

```toml
[[require]]
name = "lc-manifolds"
git = "https://github.com/glaroton/lc-manifolds"
rev = "main"
```

Then `import LocallyConvexManifolds` loads the whole library, and a single file is imported as
`LocallyConvexManifolds.Analytic.Basic`, etc. The declarations live in the namespace
`LocallyConvex.Manifolds`; those of lc-analysis, in `LocallyConvex.Analysis`.
